# REC-BMS and Solar Priority 3.3

Solar Priority restores the committed engine 4.9 decisions inside the improved REC safety and observation boundary. The consumer owns operating decisions; REC owns charger commands and the shore relay.

## Operating behavior

| State | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `shore` | Wait for sustained evidence of usable solar. |
| `probe` | Disconnect shore for the legacy 90-second MPPT ramp, clocked from the confirmed departure; evaluate battery power. |
| `solar` | Supply loads from solar/battery; return for sustained deficit, reserve, or faults. |
| `suspend` | Temporarily reconnect for a large load, then resume into `solar` when it clears. |

The `burndown` state, the harvest-and-burn cycle and the ceiling-stall burn are gone (engine 4.15, Stage B): ordinary HOLD no longer deliberately fills and burns a voltage band, and a sustained deficit in HOLD takes the ordinary return to shore.

One-way charging engages when the target is more than one SOC point above the battery and exits within half a point (engine 4.12; one function, `select_objective`, decides at startup, on a target change and every tick). On shore REC maintains the legacy sustain floor; off shore it is released so solar can charge. The floor is asked for again the moment the engine heads for shore -- as soon as the shore input is *available* (VE.Bus `/Ac/State/AcIn1Available`) or any AC input is accepted, held through a 90 s grace when the firmware publishes no availability, and released while shore is known absent so an attempted return cannot pin the MPPTs at the hold band (Stage A of the [repair plan](../reviews/solar-priority-repair-plan-2026-09-14.md), master D02/D14). Both arrays already tracking with sufficient measured capacity can enter solar directly. Its deficit return is an energy drawdown (engine 4.17): battery power below zero adds to a running deficit, power above zero pays it back, never below zero, and at `island_deficit_wh` (75 Wh, about 0.09 % SOC) the engine returns to shore; HOLD islands use the same rule, DISCHARGE keeps descending. A two-minute water-heater burst passes, a steady −49 W is bounded at 75 Wh per island, and suspend (now 2.5 kW) is the fast path for loads that matter within seconds. One-way discharge uses a ceiling and allows intentional battery discharge toward the target; the ceiling's servo steps down on any positive battery current, from the sun as much as from the Quattro, so a bright hour cannot refill a stale ceiling (master D07). Between the two, HOLD asks REC for the two-sided hold (sustain 3, Stage B): the slider target is the reference; below it the MPPTs get the solar band so the sun can finish; at the destination (REC 3.6.0) the band closes, the Quattro and the MPPTs are both commanded the hold voltage, the bank's own rest voltage, and the bank is regulated by that voltage in Victron's CC/CV sense, the published charge limit being the BMS's own (REC 3.7.0, `charge_limit_a = 0`). Under the target the voltage servo steps up while the bank drains; at and above it the arrival latch hands the bank to the finer current servo (REC 3.6.1), which lands the chargers on the loads and leaves a bank sitting above the target to them. Arriving at the destination from either side re-anchors the hold on the bank's measured rest voltage and folds the servo it wound getting there (3.3.1): the boat's first HOLD on 2026-09-14 was taken at 49.1 % for 50 %, wound +0.50 V lifting the bank, and at 49.9 % left the Quattro 0.45 V above a bank it may no longer fill, restrained only by a 0 A limit it honours slowly (2 A for minutes) and that every solar boost lifts (6 A into the held bank per boost); with the servo folded the Quattro sits on the bank with no headroom to spend. Only a servo carrying at least `arrival_fold_v` (0.25 V) is folded, and within two deadbands of the destination the hold servos like the floor, up only while the bank drains (3.3.2): folding the few hundredths that cover the loads re-anchored the hold every 30 s on the band's edge and the unconditional lift wound +0.48 V onto a bank 0.02 % under it. Arrival from either direction hands into that hold. A 100% target is not a shore bulk charge (3.3, master D12): one-way charge runs toward it like toward any target, the floor keeping its solar band, and arrival is the endgame -- COMPLETE_FULL releases every hold so both chargers are commanded the true 61.96 V (never 61.96 V less a lead) and the Quattro finishes and keeps it there. A fast charge from shore is Solar Priority switched off.

The committed thresholds are in `solar_priority.ini [engine]`. The solar need for a probe or a direct entry is REC's complete DC-bus island demand (`[control]` inverter efficiency, idle and uncertainty, plus the DC loads) from its policy snapshot, smoothed by the consumer; without fresh valid demand no elective departure is admitted, while one-way discharge still leaves (engine 4.13). Readiness is 30 seconds, probe ramp 90 seconds, engine cooldown five minutes. REC additionally preserves physical relay dwell, rate and fault guards. The engine waits for REC departure readiness before starting a probe, and the probe's ramp clock starts only once the departure is physically confirmed (ActiveInput 240) and the bank has `probe_headroom_v` of room under the effective CVL; a transfer that is not confirmed, or a bank with no headroom, within 90 s returns on the ordinary cooldown and is not a failed measurement (engine 4.14). An evaluated failed probe rides the next requests as purpose `failed_probe` until REC acknowledges it, and REC counts it once per physical departure into the durable backoff (15 min doubling to 4 h on the failures of the last 24 h; a restart cannot erase it, issue #8). Full baseline deviations are [documented here](../reviews/solar-engine-baseline-deviations.md).

**Dead-code sweep (REC 3.7.1, engine 4.23, consumer 3.5.5).** No behaviour change: only code and prose nothing could reach any more. `sustain_servo`'s two-sided-hold branch kept the 3.6.0 rule "above the destination, step down while anything fills the bank", but since 3.6.1 the arrival latch hands every hold at or above its destination to `hold_current_servo`, so that branch had no caller -- it is gone, with the test assertions and the `.ini` and README sentences that still described it as the live rule. The consumer derived a `feed_shore` engine input every tick that the engine only ever copied into a local nothing read (it compares `ActiveInput` against `FEED_NONE`): gone from `Inputs`, from the consumer and from the harness. Also removed: the dead stores `eff_cv`, `_last_offset_warn` (the D-Bus publish of the effective charge voltage is untouched), the adapter's `last_snapshot`, `last_connected` and `recovery_since`, its unused `target_voltage` tick parameter and two unused interval locals, an unused `import sys` in `solar_watch.py`, and five write-only diagnostics plus an uncalled `stop_rec` helper in the offline plant. The `_sustain_ccl` brake stays exactly as it is, off by default and configurable; the engine's blind explore probe stays for the one corner where no boost has room (a charge target at or above 61.66 V). Every weakened `ChargeLimit > 100` assertion left over from the brake's withdrawal now asserts the real invariant instead: under a hold the published limit IS the BMS's own.

**Destination servo period (REC 3.7.2).** The two-sided hold's servo at its destination steps every `hold_servo_period_s` (90 s) instead of the SOC servo's 30 s. The boat's first swept morning (2026-09-16 15:44-16:36 UTC, dim sky, the Quattro parked) showed why: between boosts the servo stepped 74 times and the bank's current was still answering the previous step at 73 of them. An MPPT takes 60-90 s to settle after a 0.01 V step and as long to restart from 0 W, so every landing overshot 2-4 steps each way: on the way down the arrays were cut to 0 W (16 % of the time), on the way up the bank drained (27 %) until the voltage sat 0.03-0.04 V over it, and the next boost froze the servo at that overshoot. One step per settling time lets each be judged on a settled current. The SOC servo keeps `servo_period_s` while the band is open. No other change.

**Drift band and the end of the probe assist (REC 3.7.3, engine 4.24, consumer 3.5.6).** The first island of the swept build (2026-09-16 18:41 UTC) showed the destination servo fighting the MPPTs' own regulation: the chargers regulate on a sense quantised to 0.01 V, so one step of the hold voltage swings the bank by a whole step of current, about 1.5 A with the Quattro and 3.7 A islanded with the MPPTs the only source, and the old band of `servo_drain_a` (0.3 A) had no reachable state inside it. The servo alternated between a step too high and a step too low for the whole island, -4.5 to +3 A every four minutes with the arrays cut to 150 W on every low step, shifting a bank that only had to sit still. `hold_drift_a` (2.5 A) is the band the bank may now sit in either way inside the SOC deadband; above the target a drift out of up to two of it is welcome and any fill over `servo_drain_a` is answered, under it any drain over `servo_drain_a` is. The SOC walks a slow triangle across its deadband, a step every half hour or so, and no array is ever cut to 0 W. Engine 4.24 releases the probe assist the tick the probe passes: its 0.30 V lift is for the arrays' ramp into the island, and left to expire it ran 150 s into the island at up to 8 A into the held bank.

## Safety and metering

Fresh raw REC limits, voltage/current, SOC, cells, temperatures and module permissions remain authoritative. Missing critical data cannot grant charge or departure permission. Final commands and intermediate voltage-base/solar-offset combinations remain within the REC and installation envelope. Voltage readback is required for a new departure; missing MPPT reports during an otherwise healthy island do not independently force shore. Observed unsafe limits still do.

A voltage pair that stays unverified for `lead_verify_s` is a regulation fault (3.0.1): `/RecBms/LeadFault` names the cause, InternalFailure warns, boosts are refused, `/RecBms/SolarLead` reads 0 and the commands stand — there is no fallback to the full slider. Quattro net DC power is fresh voltage × signed current. Its reported `/Dc/0/Power` is retained as diagnostic data, not used as net DC truth. Successful bounded asynchronous reads refresh unchanged observations. Missing, removed and stale sources lose validity. Source discovery is cached; historical ledger buckets and journals are excluded from control snapshots.

`energy_accounting.py` retains timestamped native Ah/Wh, separate charge/discharge counters, gaps, references and historical reporting. Uncertain history stays uncertain. Old energy allowances, return-energy calibration and trial status do not authorize or veto normal operation; the reservation and reverse-event lifecycle (`reserve`, `close_reservation`, `end_reverse_event`) has no caller in 3.0 and its telemetry is not a per-event guarantee. Its EFC is (charge Ah + discharge Ah) / (2 × capacity): 0.01 EFC is 28.8 Ah combined at the 1440 Ah basis, i.e. 2 % combined movement; 1 % combined movement is 14.4 Ah (14 Ah at REC's 1400 Ah). About 1 % of movement a day is a paper calculation and a monitoring target, not a runtime limit. No commissioning arm command exists in the active architecture.

## Services and protocol

- `dbus_recbms.py`, battery instance 200 and Max Charge switch 220: raw CAN, battery protection, sustain/boost primitives and publication.
- `rec_policy_adapter.py`: fresh observations, accounting, intent lease and the sole policy relay writer.
- `solar_engine.py`: pure five-state decision engine, independent of D-Bus.
- `solar_priority.py`, switch instance 221: enable/rated-PV settings, observations and engine request transport. Instance 222 stays retired.
- `policy_contract.py`: protocol v2, generation, ordered request IDs, bounded lease, relay feedback, dwell and departure limits.
- `control_watchdog.py`: successful-tick heartbeat; terminate/reap a stuck REC child before daemontools restarts it. The watchdog never writes actuators.

`/RecBms/Policy/Request` contains `version`, `generation`, `request_id`, `mode`, `target_soc`, `transfer_intent`, `requested_limits` and `lease_s`. Limits carry sustain 0/1/2/3 (release/floor/ceiling/hold), purpose and an optional bounded boost edge. Lease renewal does not renew a boost. Mode reports the engine's choice; REC does not select another mode. A generation change invalidates old requests. An expired consumer lease forces shore and retains existing sustain protection while recovering: REC's adapter keeps refreshing a retained hold, so the primitive's own 120 s expiry is not a dead-owner release. The operator's side of that loss (3.2, master D13) has four outcomes through the same path: a lost lease returns prepared under the mode's hold and keeps it at the target it was taken for; a target moved during the loss makes that hold stale, and it is released once the bank is back on an accepted input (a return in progress keeps it), after which the slider's own curve applies as it does with no Solar Priority running; OFF keeps a hold through the protected return and releases it once an input is accepted; a recovered consumer resumes through a fresh valid request.

**Shore AC input (3.4.0 / consumer 3.5.0).** Nothing hard-codes which Quattro input carries shore power any more (the owner moves shore from AC in 1 to AC in 2 on 2026-09-15 so the Quattro's own solar priority can be used). Both services resolve it with one shared rule (`policy_contract.resolve_shore_input`): a pinned `shore_ac_input = 1|2` wins; under `auto` (the default) the GX's AC input types decide when exactly one input is Grid or Shore power (`/Settings/SystemSetup/AcInput1|2`, read every 30 s); failing that the input already settled on is kept, so an island (ActiveInput 240, nothing available) never moves the REC off the input it is ignoring; a fresh start with nothing to go on reads the accepted input, then the single available input, and falls back to 1. REC persists the result in its durable transfer state, publishes it as `Policy/Telemetry/Relay/ShoreInput` and in the status document (`shore_ac_input`, with the reason), writes `/Ac/Control/IgnoreAcIn<N>` on that input only, and when the resolved input changes it releases any ignore still standing on the input it leaves. The consumer follows REC's published resolution and derives the engine's `ac_available` and shore feed value from it each tick.

**Prefer renewable energy (REC 3.5.0).** With shore on AC in 2 the Quattro runs its own solar priority: `/Dc/0/PreferRenewableEnergy` = 1 parks the charger in sustain and it never charges from shore (measured 2026-09-15: zero amps for an hour, the DC loads draining the bank at 0.8 A on shore), 0 lets it charge normally. REC owns that toggle the way it owns the relay, with one daily rhythm (`policy_contract.prefer_renewable_wanted`): prefer solar from dawn (the brightest array's voltage at least `dawn_voc_v` for `dawn_s`; array voltage, never PV current, which the hold's cap curtails on shore), charge now from dusk (every array under `dusk_voc_v` for the floor's `dusk_s`) in CHARGE and HOLD so the hold covers the DC loads and lifts the bank from shore overnight exactly as it did on AC in 1; solar all night in DISCHARGE, where the descent is the plan; and by day charge now only once the bank is `day_deficit_pct` (one point) under its floor or target, held until it is back inside the band, so a dark day costs one flip and a passing cloud none. Solar Priority off leaves the owner's own setting alone. Written only on a change and at most once a minute, read back, and published as `Policy/Telemetry/PreferRenewable` and `Daylight` and in the status document (`prefer_renewable`: wanted, actual, reason). The solar boost is 240 s with its measurement window in the last minute, because a tracker that has sat at open-circuit voltage under the hold's cap can take over two minutes to walk down to its maximum power point; the engine does not wait the full boost when the live yield already covers the need (4.20): it takes its captures at once and leaves shore after its usual 30 s confirm.

**Blind spots closed on 2026-09-15 (REC 3.4.1, consumer 3.5.1, engine 4.19).** Three things the first afternoon on AC in 2 exposed. The hold's current trim outlived its cause: a re-accept surge filled the held bank to 50.2 %, the trim wound to −2.5 A, and with the bank then draining nothing unwound it, so the published limit sat on its 0.1 A floor, the MPPTs made 6 W under a 75 V sky and the DC loads came out of the bank on shore; a trim now relaxes a step toward zero whenever the bank stops doing what it was trimmed for (the trim, and the near-zero limit it served, are gone in 3.6.0 below). Under that cap the engine's measurement boost never fired, because its `boost_min_pv_w` gate read the curtailed 6 W as darkness; with a charge current limit at or below `boost_capped_a` (3 A) the yield says nothing and the boost, which lifts the limit, is gated on daylight alone. And a connect command issued while the VE.Bus service was restarting was booked as a refused write and an hour's fault lockout; a command with no service to write to is now deferred and re-asserted, never faulted.

**The hold regulates its destination by voltage (REC 3.6.0).** The 3.2.0 hold curtailed the surplus at its destination by current: a published charge limit equal to the DC load DVCC does not compensate itself, about 1 A here, trimmed toward a 0.1 A floor. DVCC allocates such a limit as 0 A to one of the two MPPTs, and a BlueSolar given 0 A switches off, restarts its tracker cold when the limit comes back and can sit for minutes at open-circuit voltage: on 2026-09-15 array B made 40 W of its 650 W through a whole probe that way, and the boost's own expiry re-created the gap a second time. The owner's Victron guidance settles the design: the charge voltage limit is the normal top-of-charge control, the charge current limit is for genuine battery-current constraints, and a limit near zero means "stop the chargers". So at the destination, the bank within `servo_deadband_pct` under the target or anywhere above it, the solar band closes and every charger is commanded the hold voltage; arriving from below re-anchors the hold on the bank's measured rest voltage whatever the servo carries, and the latch reopens the band only two deadbands under, so a bank dithering on the edge never flips the MPPT ceiling. The charge limit is the floor's `PV + charge_limit_a` everywhere, the current trim is gone, and the servo answers a fill above the target from any source, the Quattro's few hundredths over its command or the sun through a hold voltage that ended up over the bank, with a step down; a bank sitting still above the target is left to the loads. What follows: no MPPT is switched off on shore, so no cold restarts and no minutes at open-circuit voltage; on the island PV supplies exactly the loads because the bus sits at the voltage limit and the MPPTs stay voltage-limited and tracking; by day on shore with prefer renewable energy the Quattro is parked and PV carries the DC loads at the hold voltage; at night in charge now the Quattro holds the bank at that same voltage. One behaviour to expect: in charge now by day the Quattro, which holds slightly over its command, carries the loads instead of the sun, which is why the daily prefer-renewable rhythm matters.

**The destination servo regulates on current (REC 3.6.1).** The first hour of 3.6.0 on the boat showed the SOC servo overshooting the landing: after the 23:00 UTC re-accept the arrays were filling a bank 0.2 % over target at 5 A, the "down while filling" rule stepped 0.02 V twice, the bank went from +5 A to −1 A, and the arrays sat at open-circuit voltage making 0 W under a bright sky while the DC loads came out of the bank, for the hours it would have taken 1 A of loads to bring 0.2 % down. The bank takes about 1.5 A per 0.01 V here, so a 0.02 V step is far too coarse to land the chargers on the loads. At its destination the hold now servos on the bank's own current (`hold_current_servo`): filling means the hold voltage sits over the bank, draining means under it, and the SOC error only sets what current is wanted. Inside the deadband none either way, so the chargers carry the loads and the bank sits still; above the target nothing into the bank and a drift out of up to two `servo_drain_a` is welcome, so the bank trends back without the chargers being starved; under it a drain is answered and a fill is the plan. Steps are `hold_step_v` (0.01 V) and the authority is `hold_servo_up_v` / `hold_servo_down_v` (0.10 / 0.30 V) around the anchor the arrival fold measured. A bank above its target is therefore not driven down: it drifts down at a fraction of an amp while the sun or the Quattro carry the rest, and it sits within a few tenths of a percent over the target rather than costing hours of curtailed sun.

**The published voltage is the chargers' sense (REC 3.6.2).** The first minutes of 3.6.1 showed the servo climbing 0.01 V per period from 55.63 to 55.69 V with the arrays at 0 W and the bank draining, although the bank read 55.65 V. The reason is DVCC's shared voltage sense: it hands the battery service's published voltage to every charger as its own battery voltage, and since v1.6.0 that figure was quantised to 0.05 V to spare the D-Bus. The chargers therefore regulated bang-bang in 0.05 V bands, and a 0.01 V servo step meant nothing to them until the true voltage crossed a 0.05 V boundary. `[publish] voltage_step` is 0.01 V now, the BMS's own resolution; the other telemetry steps stay. And the servo's boost freeze restarts a full period when the boost ends: a step ten seconds after a boost answered the boost's own fill still decaying from +7.5 A and cut the arrays to 0 W.

**The boost is the measurement (engine 4.22, consumer 3.5.4).** Three things the first evening on 3.6.x showed in the engine. The blind "explore" probe, which islands to measure the arrays when the engine holds no measurement, cut a running boost off at 190 W to go and find 190 W (01:24 UTC on 2026-09-16), costing a relay cycle and a failed-probe backoff; a boost lifts the MPPTs `boost_v` over the charge target, some 40 A of headroom on this bank, so it is a complete measurement and the blind probe now runs only where a boost has no room, a charge target within `boost_v` of `full_cvl_v` (61.96 V, so 61.66 V and up), where no boost of any kind is asked for either. The five-minute cooldown gate counted from a restart as if it were a transition, and the consumer restart of 22:15 UTC gated the departure through the one boost that saw 1023 W against a 587 W need; it counts from transitions only now. And at the hold's destination the arrays are voltage-limited to the loads, so only a boost can show a surplus and the boost interval is the sampling delay before the engine sees that the sun has become enough: `boost_interval_ms` is 10 minutes now, not 15, with the 240 s length kept because array B took three minutes to sweep to its maximum that evening.

**No charge-limit modulation (REC 3.7.0).** The floor's `PV + charge_limit_a` brake was built for one measured behaviour, the 0.6 to 2 kW re-absorb the Quattro did for 13 minutes on every AC re-accept with the pack above its command (2026-09-02). With the hold on the bank's own voltage the two re-accepts of 2026-09-15 went 5.2 A and 7.8 A for about ten seconds and then nothing, the voltage doing the stopping and the 5 A share overshot anyway; and by day the Quattro's own solar priority parks the charger, so the brake never touched it. What it did touch was the arrays: DVCC hands them their present current plus a share of 5 A each cycle, so every ramp on shore was rationed, and at the destination and on the island it never bound at all. The owner chose to run without any charge-limit modulation, re-accepts included, and `charge_limit_a` is 0 by default: REC publishes the BMS's own limit under every hold and `Sustain/ChargeLimit` reads none. The boost no longer has a cap to lift and the engine's capped-limit boost gate is moot. A positive `charge_limit_a` brings the brake back if a charge-now re-accept ever shows the burst again; the fixture's adversarial Quattro (0.08 V over its command, a 1.2 kW reconnect burst) still models that case, which is why some plant figures moved with this change.

**Prepared return (3.1, Stage A).** An ordinary return closes the relay only once the below-bank pair is in force: the mode's hold is anchored (a CHARGE floor, a DISCHARGE ceiling, a HOLD hold), the exact requested Quattro/solar pair is verified by readback and the current limit readback is inside the envelope. `TransferSupervisor` bounds that wait with `[control] return_prepare_s` (30 s) and then closes anyway, recording the return as unprepared (`Relay/Prepared`, `Relay/PrepareAgeSeconds`); expiry is not a fault, because the regulation fault already reports an unapplied pair. A lost lease, OFF or lost transport sources return through the same prepared path -- REC reconstructs the mode's hold itself when it returns on its own -- while a return the REC cannot vouch for (critical data lost, discharge prohibited, a charger outside the envelope) is urgent and never waits. Four relay facts stay separate: the ignore command, the Quattro's acknowledgment of it (`/Ac/State/IgnoreAcIn1`, `Relay/Acknowledged`), whether the supply is there (`/Ac/State/AcIn1Available`, `Relay/ShoreAvailable`; unknown stays unknown) and which input is accepted (`Relay/ActiveInput`: 0 = AC in 1, 1 = AC in 2). A connect command that is acknowledged while shore is absent is `shore unavailable`, not a refused write, and takes no lockout; a timeout with shore present names the stage that failed. Another accepted AC input is a charger, never an island, and blocks departures. `Actuator/CommandAcknowledged` is the readback of the pair; `Actuator/Settled` needs the signed Quattro V x I inside the positive reserve for `current_settle_s` on shore. Shutdown publishes CCL 0 and the safe pair before it hands the relay back, best effort on SIGTERM only: a crash or power loss skips it and the battery service disappears with every BMS limit it distributed. The `PV + charge_limit_a` sustain cap stays: the [boat evidence of 2026-09-14](../reviews/solar-priority-stage-a-boat-evidence-2026-09-14.md) shows a prepared return under the cap peaking at 394 W against 3 856 W for an unprepared one, and does not show what the prepared command would do without the cap (A4).

`/RecBms/Policy/Status`, `/Snapshot` and `/Telemetry/*` expose current authority, limits, source validity, relay state and measured energy. `/SolarPriority/*` exposes all five engine states, one-way mode, desired transfer and transition reasons.

## Configuration, verification and release

`config.ini` retains live battery calibration, `[sustain]`, `[solarboost]`, `[policy]` storage/topology and `[control]` metering/relay parameters. Named retired 2.x control keys are ignored for upgrade compatibility. `solar_priority.ini [engine]` restores legacy decision tunables. Existing localsettings and the latest `solar-control-state.json` must be preserved.

Run `python3 test_solar_priority.py` for the isolated engine, boundary, safety and coupled-plant suites; `python3 -m unittest test_deploy_cerbo` checks deployment tooling. Synthetic plant tests demonstrate controller behavior, not installed calibration.

Follow [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md): use only the shared `./cerbo` connection and `deploy_cerbo.py`. Back up the complete current pair before the protocol change, ship REC first, then the consumer. Roll back the complete compatible pair while retaining the newest ledger and localsettings. Never run a legacy direct relay writer with the REC policy executor.

Before ordinary operation is accepted on the boat, verify both versions, voltage/current commands, CPU/control-loop latency and CAN counter deltas, then observe actual automatic departure and return. A subsequent 24–48-hour log review evaluates everyday behavior. The [restoration report](../reviews/solar-priority-restoration-2026-09-13.md) records offline and installed verification separately.
